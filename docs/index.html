Medicare re‑prices every clinician on January 1.

In 2027 the conversion factor falls 2.31% and the floor under the work GPCI ends. Priced on what each clinician billed in 2024, 90% of the 1.14 million clinicians in CMS's public file are paid less for the same work. Look anyone up.

    Priced clinician by clinician on the services each billed in 2024, Medicare would pay $3.2 billion (−3.9%) less in 2027 for the same work; the median clinician sees −4.1%. That is deeper than CMS's table because CMS assumes modifiers B1 and B2 will be billed far more often than G2211 was. The end of the work floor alone accounts for about $536 million, in the 49 localities that lose it.

    Clinical social workers and psychologists gain the most, from practice-expense changes and the last step of the behavioral-health work increase. Orthopedic surgery and otolaryngology lose the most, from the new same-day modifier-25 reduction, the halving of the indirect practice cost index and, for joint replacement, an 18% cut per case.     Where the floor gives way

    Since 2004 no locality's work GPCI could fall below 1.0. That floor ends with 2026. In 2027, 49 of 109 Medicare payment localities have a work GPCI below 1.0, so the same service pays less there on top of the national cut.

    Why the dollar shrank

    Most of the 2027 cut isn't new. It's the end of a one-year 2.50% increase Congress set for 2026. Underneath, CMS moved value between specialties, and Congress let the work GPCI floor lapse.

    What moved the relative values

    • −2.31%

      The conversion factor

      $33.4009 becomes $32.6286. Most of that is the end of 2026's one-year 2.50% increase; the statute's +0.25% update and a −0.12% budget-neutrality factor follow. Qualifying APM participants get $32.9548 (−1.83%). CMS's specialty table leaves this out: the conversion factor updates "are not captured in the specialty impacts" (PI pp. 1971–1977).

    • −20%

      Same-day visits with modifier 25

      When an office visit billed with modifier 25 falls on the same day as a 0-day-global procedure by the same physician, or a same-specialty colleague in the group, every service except the highest-paid one is paid 20% less. CMS had proposed 50% across 0-, 10- and 90-day globals. It hits dermatology, otolaryngology and orthopedics hardest (PI pp. 402–403, 1974).

    • G2211 → B1

      The complexity add-on becomes a modifier

      G2211 ends. Office and home visits instead carry modifier B1, worth 16% of the visit's work RVUs, or B2 (32%) for Shared Savings Program ACO and LEAD participants. On a level-4 established visit B1 adds 0.31 RVU; G2211 added 0.52 (PI pp. 329, 341; CMS B1/B2 file). CMS's budget-neutrality math expects B1 and B2 to pay about $1.1 billion in 2027. In CMS's 2024 clinician file G2211 paid $414 million at 2026 rates; B1 on those same visits would pay $242 million.

    • ½

      The indirect practice cost index is halved

      CMS is removing the index it uses to spread indirect practice costs across specialties: half in 2027, all of it in 2028. A new cap limits any code's practice-expense change to 5% a year (PI p. 1974).

    • −18%

      Total hip and knee replacement

      Work RVUs for 27130 and 27447 fall from 19.11 to 16.70. With the conversion factor, the national facility payment for a total knee drops from $1,159.35 to $953.08 (CMS selected-procedures file).

    • +9.8%

      Behavioral health

      The last of four annual behavioral-health work increases and the practice-expense changes lift psychotherapy: 90837 in an office goes from $167.00 to $183.37 nationally. Clinical social workers (+11%) and psychologists (+9%) lead CMS's table (PI pp. 1973–1974).

    • 1.0 → 0.948

      The work GPCI floor ends

      Since 2004 no locality's work GPCI could be below 1.0. The statute ends that floor for services on or after January 1, 2027 (SSA §1848(e)(1)(E), as amended by Pub. L. 119-75 §6208). It isn't offset anywhere in the 2027 conversion factor. Arkansas and Mississippi fall furthest, to 0.948.

    • Not in 2027

      The efficiency adjustment

      The −2.5% cut to work RVUs for non-time-based services applied in 2026 is not repeated. CMS plans it every three years; the next is CY2029 (PI pp. 114–115).

    Thirty-five years of the conversion factor

    In 1992 the conversion factor was $31.00. In 2027 it is $32.63. Over the same years CMS's Medicare Economic Index, its own measure of the cost of running a practice, rose 104%. A 1992 conversion factor grown by the MEI would be $63.38 in 2027.

    Before 1998 Medicare had separate conversion factors for surgery, primary care and other services; the line follows the primary-care factor for 1993–1997. Years with a mid-year change use the day-weighted average. Values for 1992–1994 are from the House Ways and Means Green Book; later years from each year's final rule or CMS rate file. The 2027 MEI (2.6%) is CMS's estimate.

    Methods and sources

    How a payment is priced

    Every rate on this page is computed the way CMS computes it: (work RVU × work GPCI + practice-expense RVU × PE GPCI + malpractice RVU × MP GPCI) × conversion factor. Office lines use non-facility practice expense; facility lines use facility practice expense; a code with no non-facility value is paid at its facility value, and a code with no facility value at its non-facility value. At national GPCIs (1.0) the results match CMS's own “Impact on CY 2027 Payment for Selected Procedures” table to the cent for every code and setting in it. The page's tests check this on every build.

    • 2026: CMS's October 2026 relative value file (RVU26D) and its 2026 GPCIs, which include the 1.0 work floor. Conversion factor $33.4009 ($33.5675 for qualifying APM participants).
    • 2027: Addendum B (relative values) and Addendum E (GPCIs) of the CY2027 PFS final rule, CMS-1848-F. Conversion factor $32.6286 ($32.9548).
    • Statutory share. Nurse practitioners, physician assistants, clinical nurse specialists and dietitians are paid 85% of the fee schedule amount, and clinical social workers, counselors and marriage and family therapists 75%; the statement applies these.
    • Codes deleted for 2027 are priced through CMS's analytic crosswalk to their replacement codes.
    • G2211 ends in 2027. Office and home visits carry modifier B1 (16% of the visit's work RVUs) or, for Shared Savings Program ACO and LEAD participants, B2 (32%). The statement keeps the visits a clinician billed G2211 on and prices B1 (or B2, with the ACO box ticked) on those same visits, using the clinician's own mix of office and home visits. CMS's rate-setting instead assumes B1 and B2 on many more visits (about $1.1 billion in 2027, from the factor in column I of its 2025 utilization file), which is why CMS's specialty table shows primary care rising. Typical-practice statements have no G2211 line, because CMS's claims file for them doesn't carry one, so they show neither G2211 nor B1.
    • Modifier 25. The new 20% same-day reduction is applied at claim time, so it isn't in the RVUs. The statement shows it as one adjustment row: CMS's own average reduction for the specialty, code and setting, from the factor CMS used in rate-setting (2025 utilization file, column H). A practice's real effect depends on how often its visits and procedures fall on the same day.
    • Anesthesia services are paid by base and time units, which CMS's public file doesn't carry. Their lines keep the average allowed amount CMS reports and move it by the change in the locality's anesthesia conversion factor ($20.4976 to $20.1056 nationally). The 2026 column for an anesthesia line is the allowed amount from CMS's file year (2024), not re-priced to 2026.

    Clinician lookup

    • Services, place of service and average allowed amounts come from CMS's “Medicare Physician & Other Practitioners – by Provider and Service” file for the latest published year. CMS hides any code a clinician billed for fewer than 11 beneficiaries, so small lines are missing.
    • The file doesn't carry modifiers. For codes that can be billed as a professional or technical component, the page picks global, -26 or -TC by whichever 2026 rate in the clinician's locality is closest to the clinician's average allowed amount.
    • Locality is set from the practice ZIP code in CMS's file, using CMS's ZIP-to-locality file. A clinician who works in several localities is priced in one.
    • Drugs, lab tests and other services outside the physician fee schedule are set aside, not priced.
    • Volumes stay fixed at the clinician's last reported year. Real 2027 revenue will also move with volume, payer mix, MIPS and sequestration.

    Specialty figures

    • The specialty chart uses CMS's own impact table, so it carries CMS's B1/B2 assumption. The clinician counts and dollar totals in the text price each clinician's own services, with B1 only where G2211 was billed. The two answer different questions: what CMS's rate-setting expects, and what the same work pays.
    • CMS's impact table counts only relative-value changes. This page combines it with the conversion factor change and each specialty's average change in geographic indices. The geographic change is measured on CMS's clinician-level file: every clinician's services priced at 2027 relative values, once with 2026 GPCIs and once with 2027 GPCIs, then summed by specialty.
    • A recompute of CMS's table from CMS's 2025 utilization file reproduces it within rounding for specialties whose change doesn't depend on the G2211 swap. The page shows CMS's published figures, not the recompute.
    • Clinician-level totals: every clinician in CMS's file with a locality and at least one fee-schedule line (about 1.14 million), priced with the same code that runs the statement, at the non-QP conversion factor. CMS's file is 2024; volumes are held fixed.
    • Medicare specialty codes are grouped into CMS's impact categories using the Medicare specialty list; CMS doesn't publish its exact crosswalk. The grouping was checked against the table's allowed-charge totals.
